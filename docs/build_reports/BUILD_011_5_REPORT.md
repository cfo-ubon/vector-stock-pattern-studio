# Build 011.5 Report — Commercial Reality Check

**No new generation engine. No new rendering mechanisms. This is an
evidence-driven commercial audit only.**

## Methodology (read this before the numbers)

- **Portfolio**: all 15 `STYLE_DNA_PRESETS` × 100 fixed seeds each (`cr-1`
  through `cr-100`, no cherry-picking) = **1,500 patterns**, generated by
  `scripts/commercialRealityCheck.ts` (new, reuses the exact same
  `evaluate()`/`buildTile` pipeline every prior build's `qualityReport.ts`
  baseline used — every number below is a real, reproducible measurement,
  not an estimate).
- **Dimensions**: every dimension the brief names is mapped onto a real,
  already-computed signal in this codebase wherever one honestly exists —
  see the mapping table in §3. Two dimensions the brief asks for
  (**Originality**, **Recognition Value**) have **no real automated
  measurement anywhere in this codebase** (no reference-image dataset, no
  perceptual-hash/embedding comparison pipeline against real marketplace
  listings) — they are **not scored numerically** anywhere in this report.
  Where discussed, it is explicitly my own qualitative judgment, labeled
  as such.
- **Visual review**: for every preset, the best/median/worst tile (by
  Absolute Commercial Quality, that preset's own real ranking, not
  cherry-picked) was rendered to SVG and rasterized to PNG
  (`docs/build_reports/build_011_5_samples/`, 45 tiled + 45 close-up
  renders). I looked at ~15 of these directly (full list of which ones in
  §4) as a human-art-director-style visual pass — judging what's actually
  on screen, not the code that produced it.
- **Competitor comparison (§7)**: I do not have a scraped dataset of real
  Shutterstock/Adobe Stock/Creative Market/Etsy listings to diff pixels
  against — no such dataset exists in this environment or codebase. That
  comparison is my own design-domain judgment of how these outputs stack
  up against well-known conventions of premium botanical/surface-pattern
  design (scale hierarchy, negative space discipline, palette
  sophistication, repeat engineering). This is disclosed explicitly, not
  presented as a measured score.

## PART 4 — Real Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

### Finding #1 (SEVERE, root-caused, fully quantified): Lattice-layout presets are unfairly scored as broken

**Evidence**: `minimalBotanical` (layouts: `grid`, `gridMinimal`),
`boutiquePackaging` (`stripe`, `gridMinimal`), and `premiumTextile`
(`halfDrop`, `brick`) are the **only 3 of 15 presets whose entire declared
layout pool is drawn exclusively from `REGULAR_LATTICE_LAYOUTS`**
(`engine/hierarchy.ts`). Their Absolute Commercial Quality failure rates
are 99%, 92%, and 100% respectively — while their `heroVisibility`
(79-85), `patternBeautyScore` (70-82), and `luxuryFeeling` (85-89) all
read completely normally, in line with the rest of the portfolio.

Direct diagnostic (`applySoftPenalties`/`computeOverallScore` on 3 seeds
from each preset) shows the deduction is dominated by:
- `gridAppearance` (-20): "nearest-neighbor directions concentrate on the
  axes — reads as a grid"
- `equalSpacingDetected` (-15): "nearest-neighbor spacing is suspiciously
  uniform (mechanical, not organic)"
- `repeatedMotifOrientation` (-10): "motif rotation barely varies"
- `mechanicalComposition` (-20, stacks with the above three when all
  three fire together)

**These four penalty rules exist specifically to catch an *accidental*
mechanical-looking composition in an organic/scatter-style pattern.**
For a preset whose entire declared identity is a deliberate lattice
repeat — a minimalist icon grid, a striped packaging pattern, a
half-drop/brick textile print — "reads as a grid," "uniform spacing,"
and "rotation barely varies" are not defects, **they are the correct,
intended outcome**. `luxuryWallpaper` (34% failure) and `vintageHerbarium`
(46% failure) show the same effect partially, because each has one
lattice layout and one non-lattice layout in its pool — only the seeds
that resolve to the lattice option get penalized.

Visually (see `minimalBotanical_median_closeup.png`,
`boutiquePackaging_median_closeup.png`,
`premiumTextile_median_closeup.png`) — **all three actually look like
legitimate, sellable commercial patterns**: a clean black-silhouette
minimalist repeat, an elegant blush/gold striped packaging print, and a
professional earth-tone textile repeat. The score does not reflect what a
human buyer would see.

**This is a scoring-integrity bug, not an artistic one** — the single
most actionable, highest-confidence finding in this audit. See §7 for the
Build 012 recommendation this drives.

### Finding #2 (real, visually confirmed, not yet fully quantified): Hero motifs often don't read as visually dominant at whole-tile scale

Across the samples I inspected directly (`luxuryFloral_best`,
`luxuryFloral_median`, `darkBotanical_best`, `vintageHerbarium_worst`),
even presets with `premiumHero: true` and a hero-focused hierarchy preset
(`heroFocus`: heroScale 2.4× vs. fillerScale 0.5×) render as a fairly
uniform field of similarly-sized small motifs — no single flower reads
as "the" focal point at a glance. `editorialBotanical_best` is the one
clear counter-example in my sample: 3-4 genuinely large, layered,
color-differentiated bouquet clusters are immediately visible along a
real flow path.

The numeric `heroVisibility` score (79-97 across the portfolio) doesn't
flag this — it's composed from `heroDetailRatio`/`heroSeparation`/
`hierarchy`/`paletteContrast`, none of which directly measure "does the
rendered size delta actually read at a glance," which is closer to what
a human means by "hero visibility." This is a real, visually-observed
gap between the metric and the perceptual experience, **based on a
15-image sample, not an exhaustive measurement** — flagged honestly as a
finding worth a dedicated follow-up measurement (a real "rendered scale
delta vs. role" metric), not asserted as fully quantified the way
Finding #1 is.

### Finding #3 (real, minor): "Botanical" branding outruns botanical-category coverage

Only 6 of 15 presets (`editorialBotanical`, `luxuryFloral`,
`minimalBotanical`, `vintageHerbarium`, `darkBotanical`, plus
`scandinavianOrganic`/`softWatercolorInspired` which list `botanical` as
a secondary category) resolve to the `botanical` generator category at
all — `boutiquePackaging`, `luxuryWallpaper`, `premiumTextile`,
`kidsPlayful`, `retroOrganic`, `modernTropical`, `organicAbstract`,
`bohoFloral` use geometric/damask/tropical/organic/retro/cute generators.
This is by design (the app is a general pattern engine, not
botanical-exclusive — see `CLAUDE.md`) and not a defect in itself, but it
means only 6-8 of the 15 "commercial-grade botanical" claims in the
brief's mission statement are actually backed by the botanical
illustration pipeline specifically.

### Repeated visual problems across the portfolio (pattern-level, not preset-specific)

- **Most common failure mode**: mechanical-uniformity soft penalties
  firing on intentionally-regular layouts (Finding #1) — accounts for
  the overwhelming majority of all failed tiles in the 1,500-pattern run.
- **What still looks procedural**: filler/accent layers (small dots,
  crosses, diamonds, rings) are visually identical in role across nearly
  every preset regardless of category — real, useful texture, but
  recognizable as "the same background filler system" once you've seen
  it in 3-4 presets back to back.
- **What still looks too mathematical**: the lattice-layout presets, by
  the same mechanism as Finding #1 — genuinely, visibly regular in a way
  that (for the *wrong* target genre) would read as an obviously
  auto-generated repeat. For their *actual* target genre (minimalist
  grid, packaging stripe, textile brick), this reads as intentional
  engineering, not a flaw.
- **What still looks empty**: `editorialBotanical`'s airy/heroFlow
  samples lean quite sparse — large stretches of flat cream background
  between hero clusters. Appropriate for a "quiet luxury" positioning,
  risky for a buyer expecting a fuller print.
- **What still looks repetitive**: none of the visually-inspected samples
  showed obvious *adjacent* motif repetition (the `adjacencyRepetition`
  metric backs this up — portfolio mean 88.9/100) — this specific failure
  mode is not currently a real problem.
- **What still looks unnatural**: the "flower" silhouettes in ditsy/
  small-scale contexts (Finding #2's samples) read more like abstract
  stars/sparkles than recognizable botanical forms at small scale — a
  real illustration-fidelity gap distinct from Finding #2's
  scale-hierarchy issue.

## PART 7 — Build 012 Recommendation

**Build 012: Lattice-Layout Scoring Integrity Fix.**

Solves the single largest, most confidently-quantified weakness this
audit found (Finding #1): 3 of 15 Style DNA presets — real, visually
legitimate commercial patterns — are scored as near-total failures
(92-100% failure rate) purely because the fixed `stockClean` quality
weighting's grid/uniformity soft penalties don't distinguish "accidental
mechanical composition" from "deliberate lattice repeat, working exactly
as designed." This is the highest-commercial-impact, lowest-risk fix
available: it touches no generation/rendering code, is fully explainable
by the diagnostic already run in this report, and directly unblocks the
worst-scoring 20% of the product catalog from ever being fairly
represented in a portfolio evaluation, a quality gate, or (eventually) a
user-facing quality indicator.

Recommended scope: add a layout-aware exemption or a dedicated
`QualityPresetId` (e.g. reuse `computeOverallScore`'s existing
`presetId` parameter, selecting a variant weighting when the tile's own
resolved `layoutId` is in `REGULAR_LATTICE_LAYOUTS`) so `gridAppearance`/
`equalSpacingDetected`/`repeatedMotifOrientation`/`mechanicalComposition`
stop firing against patterns whose entire design intent is a regular
grid/stripe/brick repeat — while continuing to fire, exactly as today,
against scatter/organic/hero layouts where a mechanical-looking result
really is a defect.

Build 011.5 does **not** implement this fix — per the brief, this is an
audit only. Build 012 should.

## PART 8 — Commercial & Release Readiness

- **Commercial readiness**: 12 of 15 presets (80%) are honestly
  competitive at a "similar to premium tier" or "below premium tier but
  close" level today. 3 of 15 (20%) are artificially blocked by a scoring
  bug, not an artistic one — their real visual quality is comparable to
  the other 12.
- **Release readiness**: safe to ship as-is (nothing in this audit found
  a regression or a broken mechanism) — but any user-facing "quality
  score" or "commercial readiness" indicator that surfaces
  `absoluteCommercialQuality` directly should **not** be trusted for
  Minimal Botanical / Boutique Packaging / Premium Textile until Build
  012 ships, since it will misrepresent genuinely sellable output as
  near-worthless.
- **Overall verdict**: **below premium tier, closer than the raw numbers
  suggest.** The honest gap to top Shutterstock/Adobe Stock/Creative
  Market contributors is concentrated in hero-scale dominance (Finding
  #2) and small-scale illustration fidelity, not a wholesale quality
  deficit — and one of the three "failing" presets in this report
  (Minimal Botanical) is arguably already competitive in its specific
  minimalist-grid sub-genre.
